# FILM LOOK EXTENSIONS

A catalogue of feature-film cinematography building blocks plus a set of named
"house looks". The multi-shot planner folds this into every shot's H3 prompt as a
final pass, together with the camera-move library and the drafted shot itself, to
push the render toward Hollywood-feature quality.

**This is not text to paste.** It is a palette. Pick what the shot needs, translate
it into plain visual language inside the description, and obey every rule in the H3
specification above it — no keyword stacking, no camera-rig words as scene content,
no on-screen text, no cramped rooms in vertical, one beat per second, dialogue once.

Choose ONE house look for the whole video and hold it across every shot. Everything
below serves the story on screen; never add a person, a prop or an action just to
hit a look.

---

## 1. FORMAT & CAPTURE

- **Large-format clarity** — a big, clean image with smooth roll-off in the
  highlights and fine, even grain; skin and texture hold detail into the shadows.
- **Anamorphic feel** — a slightly wider, more compressed sense of space, gentle
  edge softness, oval out-of-focus highlights, a faint horizontal streak on the
  brightest points, subtle barrel bend on straight lines near frame edge.
- **Spherical / naturalistic** — no streak, rounder background blur, straighter
  lines; reads as documentary-true.
- **Film-stock character** — a soft toe in the blacks (they lift very slightly
  rather than clipping to pure black), a gentle shoulder in the highlights, a thin
  layer of moving grain, faint halation blooming around bright practicals.
- **Shutter feel** — a natural motion blur on fast movement; for tension, a crisper,
  harder edge on motion; never strobing.
- **Frame** — state 9:16 vertical when that is the target and compose for it: the
  subject placed against real vertical depth, headroom above, foreground low in
  frame.

## 2. LENS LANGUAGE

- **14–24 mm (very wide)** — vast space, deep focus front to back, strong
  foreground, faces distort if close; use for scale and isolation in a big room or
  landscape.
- **28–35 mm (wide)** — the "you are there" lens; keeps the subject in their world,
  still deep, minimal distortion at a normal distance.
- **40–50 mm (normal)** — how the eye sees; neutral, honest, no editorial.
- **75–100 mm (long)** — compresses depth, lifts the subject off a soft background,
  intimate; the face reads calm and monumental.
- **135 mm+ (very long)** — stacks the background right up behind the subject,
  isolates a detail, a hand, an eye, a blade.
- **Depth of field** — shallow (background melts, one plane sharp) for intimacy and
  dread; deep (everything sharp) for scale, geography, unease that something is
  hiding in plain sight.
- **Focus moves** — a rack focus shifts the sharp plane from one subject to another
  within the shot to redirect the eye; hold focus locked when stillness is the point.

## 3. LIGHTING

- **One motivated source** — every key light comes from something real in the room:
  a window, a lamp, a monitor bank, a fire, a car headlight, a phone. Name the
  source, its direction, its colour, its hardness. Everything not lit by it falls
  off toward black.
- **Contrast ratio** — high contrast (deep, near-black shadow beside a bright key)
  for threat and drama; low contrast (soft, filled, even) for safety, daylight,
  comedy.
- **Hard vs soft** — hard light (a bare bulb, direct sun) throws sharp-edged
  shadows and picks out texture and sweat; soft light (an overcast sky, a bounced
  source) wraps gently and flatters.
- **Direction** — side light for form and tension; back / rim light to cut the
  subject off the background with a bright edge; top light for isolation and guilt;
  under-light for wrongness; frontal for flatness and exposure.
- **Practicals in frame** — let bulbs, screens, signs and flames actually appear in
  shot and bloom slightly; they sell the world.
- **Negative fill** — a dark side to the face, shadow allowed to stay shadow.
- **Colour of light** — warm tungsten interior against cool blue night outside; the
  green cast of fluorescent tubes; the amber-pink of sodium street lamps; the hard
  neutral white of daylight; firelight flickering warm and low.
- **Volumetrics** — a light haze in the air so beams, shafts and pools of light
  become visible and the room gains depth.

## 4. COLOUR & GRADE

- **Palettes** — teal-and-amber (cool shadows, warm skin); desaturated earth (mud,
  slate, bone, oxblood); near-monochrome cool (steel, ash, a single colour accent);
  jewel-tone (deep saturated reds, golds, emeralds against black); bleach-bypass
  (silver, low colour, crushed contrast, harsh).
- **Grade** — crushed blacks with a faint filmic lift so shadow detail survives;
  controlled highlights that roll off rather than clip; a slight split-tone (cool
  shadow, warm highlight); halation around lights.
- **Skin** — kept warm and lifelike even inside a cold-graded frame.
- **Accent colour** — one colour allowed to stay pure (a red coat, a green screen
  glow, a gold flame) while the rest of the frame is held back.

## 5. ATMOSPHERE & FX

Atmospheric haze, drifting fog, fine mist, thin smoke, steam off a grate, dust
motes turning in a shaft of light, embers rising, ash falling, rain streaking and
beading and dripping, wet asphalt holding the light, snow drifting, condensation on
glass, breath fogging in cold air, spray kicked up by movement, god-rays through a
window or canopy. Use one or two, motivated by the location, to add depth and
weight — never a wall of effects.

## 6. COMPOSITION & BLOCKING

- **Stage in depth** — put someone or something in the foreground, the subject in
  the mid-ground, and a lit background beyond; move actors toward and away from the
  lens, not just left and right.
- **Foreground frame** — shoot past a doorway, a shoulder, a rail, a plant, so the
  audience feels placed in the space and slightly hidden.
- **Negative space** — leave deliberate empty frame beside or above the subject;
  let the emptiness carry the threat.
- **Symmetry** — centre the subject in a balanced, architectural frame for control,
  ritual, inevitability; break symmetry when control breaks.
- **Eyelines** — every person in frame gets ONE named gaze target that is really
  in the scene (a screen, a doorway, another person, an object, a fixed point in
  the dark). Write it. A face left with no stated eyeline drifts to the lens on
  the render — the single most common tell. Nobody looks at camera unless the
  story is a direct address; a subject who should have their back to us is written
  "seen from behind, he does not turn".
- **Held stillness is a beat** — "he freezes", "she goes rigid", "no one moves"
  is its own sentence with a rough duration ("holds for about two seconds, no
  turn, no step, shoulders locked"), not a clause tucked into a moving beat, or
  the render skips it.
- **Blocking in depth with a path** — when anyone crosses the room or handles
  another body, name the route and the contact: *around* the near side of the
  desk, one hand on the chair back, lowering the weight onto the floor. Movement
  toward and away from the lens, not just left/right.
- **Silhouette** — a figure read as a black shape against a brighter field.
- **One move per beat** — the camera makes at most one clear move per shot,
  motivated, and settles. A locked shot is stated as locked ("no pan, tilt, zoom
  or push").

## 7. SCORE & SOUND DESIGN

- **Score** — name real instruments and their behaviour: a low sustained string
  pad that swells and drops out; a single piano figure, sparse and slow; a pulsing
  low synth arpeggio; a lone horn; a deep organ; a resonating metallic drone; a
  driving percussive electronic bed. No mood words on their own.
- **Sound design** — build the room from layers: room tone always present, the hum
  of a fixture, distant traffic on wet road, fabric, footsteps on a specific
  surface, a clock, a fridge, wind finding a gap. A single hard effect lands on the
  key visible moment (a door, a blade, a body, a light cutting out).
- **Silence** — cut the score and thin the room to almost nothing for one beat
  before a shock; the drop is the effect.
- **Sub-bass** — a felt low-end pressure under dread and under scale.

## 8. HOUSE LOOKS

Each is a whole-video look. Pick the one that fits the material (or a deliberate
hybrid of two) and weave its lens, light, palette, grade, atmosphere and
score/sound into every shot.

### Game of Thrones — grounded fantasy realism
Naturalistic light only: hard low sun and deep sky-shadow outdoors, firelight and
narrow window light indoors, candles and braziers as visible practicals. Palette of
mud, stone, steel, bone and oxblood, low saturation, cold in the north and warm-dim
in the halls. Anamorphic wide lenses for landscape and court, deeper focus, the
camera loose and slightly handheld in close dialogue, locked and grand for the
establishing vista. Haze and smoke in every torchlit interior. Score: low strings
and a lone cello theme, a deep drum, sparse. Sound: wind, ravens, timber, mail and
leather, fire.

### The Matrix — the green simulation
Inside the constructed world every frame carries a faint green cast, as if lit
through old monitor glass. Hard directional light, high contrast, cool near-black
shadows, a sheen on wet streets and dark coats. Wide and normal lenses, precise
symmetrical framing, the camera capable of an impossibly smooth arc that seems to
freeze a moment and travel around it. Rain, CRT glow, dust in shafts of light.
Palette: bottle-green, black, gunmetal, one cold highlight. Score: driving
orchestral-industrial percussion and low synth. Sound: bullet casings, dial tones,
concrete, a deep digital rumble.

### TRON: Legacy — emissive dark
Environments close to pure black, everything shaped by light that comes from the
objects themselves: thin cyan and warm-orange edge-lines, glowing seams, back-lit
fog. Glossy black floor holding perfect reflections. Minimal hard-edged geometry,
huge negative space. Wide lenses, slow deliberate moves, heavy bloom on every
emissive line, thick volumetric haze. Palette: black, ice-cyan, ember-orange,
white. Score: a pulsing analog-synth arpeggio over long orchestral swells. Sound:
low hums, clean digital impacts, near-silence between them.

### Avatar — bioluminescent wild
Lush, saturated, high-key by day — deep greens, jade water, warm skin — and by
night lit from within by glowing plant-light in cyan, violet and pink. Deep
atmospheric haze stacking the jungle into layers, floating spores and pollen
catching light, shafts through the canopy. Wide lenses, deep focus, the camera
gliding and craning through real depth. Palette: emerald, teal, violet,
gold. Score: ethnic percussion, wordless voice, sweeping strings. Sound: dense
living jungle, wingbeats, water, breath.

### The Odyssey — large-format sunlit myth
Bronze-age epic under a hard Mediterranean sun: a single blazing key, deep clean
sky-shadow, sky and sea in true deep blue, skin and bronze and rope and timber
holding fine texture in a big clean image. Wide vistas, monumental centred
blocking, minimal coverage, the camera locked or a single slow deliberate move.
Salt haze on the horizon, spray, dust, low fire at night. Palette: sun-bleached
stone, ocean blue, bronze, blood-red. Score: a low horn and a hammering war drum,
choir on the vista. Sound: wind, sea, wood under strain, distant gulls, then
silence.

### Interstellar — naturalistic and vast
One hard source and no fill: raw sun, a single work-lamp, a helmet light against
total black. Muted, almost colourless palette with warm skin; dust and fine grain
in the air; wide anamorphic vistas next to tight, quiet interiors. The camera
handheld and human up close, locked and enormous on the vista, mounted so the
world rotates around a still subject. Crushed contrast, halation on every light.
Score: a church organ building from a whisper to a wall of sound, a ticking
ostinato. Sound: breath in a suit, hull ticks, wind and dust, then vacuum silence.

### John Wick — neon noir
Night, rain, and colour pools: hard rim light in magenta, cyan and cold teal
carving the figure off a black background, wet asphalt and glass throwing the
colour back. High-key enough to read every move — the action staged wide, locked
or smoothly tracking, in long unbroken takes, symmetrical and precise. Saturated
neon against deep black, one warm skin tone held through it. Practical signs and
headlights blooming. Score: a driving percussive electronic pulse with a low
melodic synth. Sound: precise mechanical clicks, glass, breath, impacts, bass.

## 9. ANTI-PATTERNS — never do these

- Stacking look words as a keyword list or a trailing tag block. It goes in the
  sentence, as description.
- Naming a camera rig, a format, a lens or a film in the prompt itself
  ("anamorphic 35 mm", "shot on Alexa", "Interstellar-style"). Describe the
  *result* — the compression, the streak, the grain — not the gear.
- Adding beats, characters, props or events to satisfy a look. The look is texture
  on the beats already there.
- On-screen text, titles, captions, watermarks, letterboxing bars.
- Cramped, boxed-in rooms in vertical delivery.
- Contradicting the drafted shot's story, blocking or dialogue. The look serves the
  shot, never the reverse.
- Changing the look from shot to shot. One look, whole video.
- Softening or dropping a staging constraint (an eyeline, a "seen from behind", a
  timed freeze, a "goes around the chair" path, a legible-text restriction) to fit
  the look or the move. Blocking is load-bearing; the palette is not.
- Reaching for a long lens or a zoom to get a two-figure contact beat tight. H3
  widens those. Stage them close to a locked or pushed-in camera instead.

## 10. STAGING & PHYSICAL TRUTH — the shot has to be *true* before it can look good

The render only honours what the prompt pins down; whatever is left open changes
every seed. For every shot, the description must carry:

- **Stage plan** — where the camera is (height, distance, what it faces) and where
  every person and key object sits, given as positions against the furniture and
  walls. Name the floor under the subject, what is directly behind them, and the
  nearest large object's colour and material.
- **Presence** — any figure the beat refers to is physically in frame, at a stated
  spot and distance, from the first moment. "Behind him", "beside her", "over the
  body" means *in shot, there*. The only exception: a figure seen on a screen or
  in a reflection only — and then the prompt says exactly that.
- **Eyeline per person** — one named in-scene target each, written as a clause.
  Never the lens unless it is a direct address. A back-to-camera subject is
  "seen from behind, does not turn".
- **Beats on the second grid** — a 5 s shot is 3–5 beats. A held state is its own
  timed sentence.
- **Solidity & contact** — bodies and objects are solid; a mover goes *around*
  furniture on a named path; nothing passes through a chair, desk, wall, door or
  body; a lift or a grab takes real weight and the furniture reacts.
- **Anatomy & surfaces** — five fingers, attached hands, one head, stable faces
  between beats; every screen and surface clean of watermark, sparkle, UI,
  caption or stray text unless the script placed it.
- **Critical legible text** — a label, a stamp, a sign the viewer must read gets
  its own sentence: held close, filling the centre of frame, sharp, exact
  characters in quotes, shown once, not warped, not duplicated. (Assume it is a
  coin-flip anyway and keep it short.)
- **Continuity capsule** — a few concrete words restating each recurring person,
  place and prop's fixed look, every shot, because the renderer sees only this
  one.
